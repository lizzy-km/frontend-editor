“The Chief’s job is simple. Take care of the Sailors, and the Sailors will take care of the mission.” Master Chief Okafor, to every new Chief he pinned

Daniel Okafor enlisted in the summer of 1996 with a duffel bag and a promise to his mother that he would write home every week. He kept that promise through six deployments, four ships, and more than a few ports whose names he still can’t pronounce.

He made Chief in 2009 and spent the next seventeen years doing what Chiefs do: turning young Sailors into leaders. Hundreds of them now wear khakis of their own. Many will be in the room on December 4th.

His family, shipmates, and friends invite you to celebrate a career built on steady hands, straight talk, and a coffee mug that has never once been washed.

Ship’s log

Thirty years at sea and ashore, in the order it happened.

  1. 1996

    Recruit Training, Great Lakes

    Enlists as a Seaman Recruit. Graduates as an honor recruit and reports to Boatswain’s Mate “A” School.

  2. 1998

    First ship, first deployment

    Deck division aboard a guided-missile destroyer. Earns his Enlisted Surface Warfare pin in eleven months.

  3. 2003

    Petty Officer First Class

    Leads a deck division of forty Sailors through two back-to-back deployments.

  4. 2009

    Pinned Chief Petty Officer

    Anchors pinned by his wife and his father. Assigned as Leading Chief of a 300-Sailor department.

  5. 2014

    Senior Chief, Recruit Training Command

    Instructs and mentors more than 4,000 recruits over three years.

  6. 2018

    Master Chief Petty Officer

    Selected to the top one percent of the enlisted force.

  7. 2021

    Command Master Chief

    Senior enlisted leader of an amphibious assault ship with a crew of 1,200.

  8. 2026

    Fair winds and following seas

    Retires after thirty years. Plans to fish, coach his grandson’s soccer team, and finally learn to cook.

The flag passing

One of the Navy’s most moving traditions. A folded flag is passed from the most junior Sailor present up through every rank the retiree held, until it reaches him.

Each hand that holds it stands for a year he stood the watch, a rank he earned, and a Sailor he brought along behind him.

The last pair of hands is his own.

Guests are asked to remain seated and silent during the passing.
